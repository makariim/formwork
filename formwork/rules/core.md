# Core rules

Thirteen. These come up in almost every piece of work.

Each says what it catches. None tells you a story. The stories belong to
somebody else's project, and a class of failure is more useful than one
instance of it anyway.

None of these is blocked by a program. They are advice. What is blocked is
listed in `FORMWORK.md`.

---

### Do one authorised piece of work, then stop

**Advice.**

Finish the piece in front of you. Then halt, even when the following one looks
obvious, or small, or more convenient to fold in now. Halt and report.

If a brief contains two pieces of work, say so before starting.

**Catches:** work that runs past its edge. The extra part is the part nobody
asked for, and the reviewer is looking at the part they did ask for, so nobody
reads it.

---

### The agent never decides

**Advice.**

An agent may write down a decision you have already made, word for word. It may
not make one.

Work that turns out to need a decision halts and surfaces two things: the subject of
the decision, and which later work turns on it.

A decision is a fork where the other road produces different work later, and
editing will not get you back. Everything else is just a choice. Make it and
carry on.

**Catches:** a choice made in passing that nobody reviewed, and that becomes
load-bearing before anyone notices a choice was made at all.

---

### Outside your job: name who owns it, stop

**Advice.**

Found a problem that is not yours? Say so, say whose it is, and stop. Do not fix
it. Do not answer it briefly first.

**Catches:** a fix made outside the scope, which is invisible in review because
the reviewer is reading the thing that was requested.

---

### A number comes with the command that made it

**Advice.**

Every figure arrives with the command that produced it, and with what would make
it wrong. Never type a new number over an old one. Run the thing again.

**Catches:** a number nobody can reproduce. It looks exactly like a number
somebody remembered, and there is no way to tell them apart later.

---

### The right shape is not the right answer

**Advice.**

A field being filled in is not the field being correct. A file existing is not
the file being right.

**Catches:** a check that passes for the wrong reason. These are cheap to write,
which is why there are so many of them.

---

### Finding nothing tells you about your search

**Advice.**

Three things decide what a search saw: what the tool quietly skipped, the word
you typed, and where you pointed it.

Change one, leave the other two, and you have proved nothing.

Before saying something does not exist, say which of the three you changed. Then
change the others, or admit you did not.

**Catches:** "it is not there" when it is there, under a different word, in a
folder you did not look in, or in a file your tool skipped without saying so.

---

### Not measured means not measured

**Advice.**

If you did not measure it, write NOT ESTABLISHED. Not "roughly". Not "probably".

**Catches:** a guess that looks like a measurement a month later, when nobody
remembers which it was.

---

### A brief has six headings

**Advice.**

Goal. Scope. What must not happen. How we know it is done. How it gets checked.
What the report must contain.

Six, and no others. A heading you leave out is the one that gets forgotten.

For a task-sized piece of work, one line is enough. Scale the paperwork to the
work, or the work stops happening.

**Catches:** the brief that made sense to whoever wrote it and nobody else.

---

### A report answers the same questions every time

**Advice.**

List each altered file with a one-line reason. List every command that wrote to
disk. Give the check result, and `git status`, which shows nothing was staged.

Then the three that matter:

- work you did beyond the request, with the reason
- work in the request you left undone, with the reason
- anywhere the brief turned out to be inaccurate

**Catches:** a report shaped to whatever happened, which cannot be compared
with the last one. The same headings every time are what let you notice that
the third line is missing again.

---

### Say the contradiction first

**Advice.**

Put anything that conflicts with what you were told first, where it cannot be
missed. Where somebody described a problem inaccurately, state that directly,
and state it early.

**Catches:** the most valuable thing an agent produces. The discovery that the
person directing it has the wrong picture. Arriving on page four where nobody
reads it.

---

### Say you were wrong, do not fold it in

**Advice.**

When an earlier answer was wrong, say it was wrong. Do not quietly absorb the
correction into new text.

**Catches:** a reader who never learns that the earlier answer was held, and so
never learns what to distrust.

---

### Prefer the smaller claim that survives

**Advice.**

A confident wrong answer costs more than a slow one. Given two claims, make the
narrower one that holds up rather than the wider one that does not.

**Catches:** overclaiming, which is the hardest failure to spot from outside,
because it reads as competence.

---

### Your mistakes are yours to learn

**Advice.**

Keep your own list of the mistakes your project actually makes. Do not copy
anybody else's, including the ones behind these rules.

**Catches:** a borrowed list makes you watch for problems you do not have, and
worse, makes you feel safe about the problems you do have.

*This rule is why the others have no stories attached.*
